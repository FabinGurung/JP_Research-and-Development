Abstract

Research summary

The Architecture, Engineering, and Construction sector in Nepal still depends heavily on fragmented digital workflows, where architectural drawings, structural calculations, quantity estimates, method statements, specifications, and site-related records are commonly prepared in separate software tools or spreadsheets. This separation creates repeated data entry, inconsistent project information, weak traceability, and difficulty in reusing the same building data across different stages of design and construction.

This study addresses the need for an integrated data-centered workflow by designing and implementing a normalized relational database system that connects BIM and GIS concepts with automated building design and construction workflow processes. PostgreSQL/Supabase was used as the database development platform. The prototype was developed around a shared element and dimension hub, where project points, building elements, dimensional parameters, units, and element relationships were stored in normalized relational tables. The same shared data were then reused for structural analysis, architecture line-view generation, concrete quantity estimation, construction work-item linkage, method statement linkage, specification linkage, reporting, and validation.

A database-driven Moment Distribution Method module was implemented using a two-span continuous beam example to demonstrate structural calculation automation. The system calculated fixed end moments, stiffness, distribution factors, joint balancing, carry-over moments, final end moments, reactions, and BMD/SFD points. The prototype also generated architectural line-view data and concrete quantity outputs from the same beam dimensions. The results show that a normalized relational database can serve as a single source of truth for connecting design, analysis, estimation, and construction documentation workflows. The study concludes that such a database-centered approach can improve consistency, reduce repeated manual work, and provide a foundation for future BIM-GIS-enabled construction automation in Nepal.

Conclusions

What the study supports

The conclusion remains bounded to the normalized prototype and its controlled sample.

01

The prototype demonstrates that normalized relational data can act as a central backbone for selected AEC workflows.

02

Common project data were stored once and reused for structural analysis, architecture representation, estimation, and construction documentation.

03

The database-driven MDM result reached joint equilibrium and passed the implemented convergence and health checks.

04

The work establishes a foundation for future BIM/IFC and GIS/PostGIS integration rather than claiming a complete commercial BIM-GIS platform.
