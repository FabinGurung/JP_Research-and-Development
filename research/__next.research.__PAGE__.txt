2:T99d,In the Nepalese AEC sector, building project information is commonly distributed across multiple disconnected documents and software tools. Architectural drawings, structural calculations, quantity estimates, method statements, specifications, and site records are often prepared by different professionals using different formats. This fragmented system makes it difficult to maintain consistency across the complete project lifecycle. A major problem arises when the same information is required in several project stages. A member dimension may be used in architectural layout, structural analysis, quantity calculation, and construction documentation. In a manual workflow, this dimension is often copied from one document to another. If the design changes, each copy must be updated separately. This creates a high possibility of mismatch between drawings, calculations, quantities, and site documents. The lack of a single source of truth also limits automation. Even when BIM or GIS tools are available, the absence of a normalized database structure makes it difficult to connect building objects, spatial data, design parameters, construction quantities, and documentation rules in a consistent way. Without a relational backbone, BIM data may remain isolated in modeling software, GIS data may remain isolated in spatial files, and estimation or structural data may remain locked in spreadsheets or calculation sheets. In the context of Nepal, this issue is more critical because building code implementation, seismic design requirements, municipal approval processes, and construction quality control require consistent and reliable information. Studies on building-code compliance and seismic vulnerability in Nepal indicate that safe construction practice depends not only on the existence of standards but also on their consistent implementation in design and construction workflows . Manual and fragmented information management makes this consistency difficult to achieve. Therefore, the problem addressed by this research is the absence of a normalized, integrated, and reusable database framework capable of connecting BIM, GIS, structural analysis, estimation, method statements, and specifications within a single AEC workflow.
