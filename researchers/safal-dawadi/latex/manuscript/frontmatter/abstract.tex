\begingroup\setlength{\parindent}{2em}
Construction rework refers to removing, repairing or repeating work already carried out. In this study, I examined workmanship and rework-related conditions encountered during residential-building construction and maintenance in Pokhara Metropolitan City. The discussion focuses on waterproofing, plaster and brick masonry. I drew upon my construction-site observations, 17 selected photographs relevant to these activities, and published research on construction rework and engineering practice.

At a residential house in Lamachur, I observed work associated with leakage at a door threshold, including exposed construction details and corrective intervention affecting previously completed finishes. Other photographs illustrate roof and parapet finishes and masonry interfaces. I compared the observed conditions with possible construction mechanisms and technical recommendations without assigning causes solely from photographs.

The observations highlight the importance of waterproofing continuity, wall connections, suitable material preparation, drainage and curing. They also demonstrate the distinction between a visible condition and documented corrective work. Although site diaries and cost records have been reported as available, individual records have not been fully reconciled in this manuscript; therefore, no project-wide rework frequency, total cost or completion delay is claimed. The findings are a qualitative assessment of selected building-site conditions rather than a statistical account of all rework in Pokhara.
