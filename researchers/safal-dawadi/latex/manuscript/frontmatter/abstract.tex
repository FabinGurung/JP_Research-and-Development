\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}
Construction rework involves correcting, removing, replacing or repeating work that has already been executed. This thesis examines practitioner-reported construction-quality concerns associated with reinforced-concrete building projects in Pokhara Metropolitan City in three subject areas: waterproofing defects, plain-cement-concrete (PCC) pavement cracking, and plaster cracking associated with masonry workmanship. The qualitative material was supplied through the project lead from a reported engineering-practice background. The original mixed Nepali--English account and edited translation are preserved in the project research record, but the observer's identity and individual project documentation still require confirmation; the material is therefore treated as practitioner-reported evidence rather than a verified series of measured cases.

The study interprets concerns about omitted wet-area waterproofing, terrace falls, penetrations, tile joints and membrane compatibility; PCC pavement cracking, curing and reinforcement misconceptions; and wall junctions, opening bands, brick moisture conditioning and mortar curing. Technical interpretations are compared with an existing ten-paper scholarly rework bibliography and identifiable construction guidance. The analysis distinguishes a reported condition from a plausible mechanism, a confirmed cause, documented corrective action and any attributable cost or schedule effect. In particular, absence of distributed reinforcement does not automatically indicate defective jointed plain concrete pavement, and detailed wall-band requirements depend on the applicable structural system and approved design.

The findings provide practical recommendations for inspections before concealed work is covered, coordinated detailing, curing, material preparation and event documentation. They do not establish project-level rework frequencies, measured losses or delays, because the necessary corroborated field and financial records have not been supplied. The resulting assessment offers a technically grounded foundation for subsequent verified case investigation without treating unconfirmed accounts as numerical results.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} construction rework; waterproofing; PCC pavement; plaster cracking; brick masonry; site observation; Pokhara Metropolitan City.
