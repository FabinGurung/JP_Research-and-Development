\chapter{CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS}
\section{Conclusions}
During construction-related work in Pokhara, I observed or reviewed waterproofing details, masonry junctions and plaster conditions that warranted workmanship checks. The photographs of the Lamachur residential-house threshold show conditions associated with corrective waterproofing work. Other building photographs show roof, parapet and masonry interfaces at separate locations. These observations support a qualitative engineering assessment; they do not independently establish the frequency of rework or its total cost across the city.

The technical discussion highlights the importance of continuity at waterproofing transitions, appropriate drainage, sound substrate preparation, and correct masonry wall connections and material conditioning. Visible dampness or cracks can have more than one possible explanation. A diagnosis requires evidence from the actual construction details, dates, inspections and, where necessary, tests.

Across both building-work subjects, inspection before concealment or finishing is an important practical quality-control measure. Reviewing membrane continuity before tiling and masonry connections before plastering may make discrepancies easier to identify and correct. Published construction-rework research likewise emphasizes control, coordination and record keeping \parencite{forcada2017,safapour2019,love2022}. Because the source records have not been reconciled for every illustrated event, no measured monetary loss, rework percentage or project-completion delay is presented.

\section{Recommendations for Practice}
\subsection{Waterproofing}
Follow the approved building waterproofing detail and compatible manufacturer requirements for the particular wet-area or roof assembly. Inspect substrate preparation, drainage slopes, membrane junctions, service penetrations and thresholds before finishes conceal them. Record checks, tests when specified and subsequent repairs with dates and photographs. Tiling and grout should not be relied upon as substitutes for the designed waterproofing system.

\subsection{Plaster and masonry}
Inspect intersecting-wall connections against the approved drawings. Verify the detailing requirements applicable to the actual building system, including reinforcement around openings where specified. Check the condition and moisture absorption of brick units, mortar preparation, curing and plaster substrate before application. Investigate movement or workmanship issues before covering a crack with a cosmetic repair.

\section{Recommendations for Research Documentation}
For each suspected rework event, I recommend preserving the site identity in an authorized private record while using anonymized references in the report. The record should identify the original executed work, observed condition, date, actual corrective action and supporting photographs. Where available, drawings, site diaries, repair instructions, measured quantities and relevant labor or cost records should be cross-referenced rather than inferred from image appearance.

A separate assessment is required for the physical cause and for any additional direct cost or schedule impact. The research should not count a construction defect as rework when no correction has been established.

\section{Limitations and Further Work}
This report uses 17 building-related photographs, my reported field observations and selected technical literature. The available material has not been independently reconciled into a complete event-level measurement and impact dataset. I therefore present a bounded qualitative study rather than numerical claims about the wider construction industry in Pokhara. Further work should check original site diaries, drawings and repair documentation, confirm case eligibility and compare verified corrective events where adequate evidence exists.
