\chapter{Conclusions and Recommendations}

\section{Conclusions}
The representative building designed using the Draft Final NBC 205:2012 provisions showed more concrete design-check deficiencies as the adopted NBC 105:2025 demand increased. The available checks identify 1 failing frame at Soil C/$Z=0.25$ and 21 failing frames at Soil C/$Z=0.35$ and $0.40$. These findings support reassessment of the investigated historical design under current seismic demand; they do not establish a failure rate for all buildings designed under the 2012 guidance.

For the matched NBC 205:2024 comparison, the Regular configuration provides the reference response. Under Soil C/$Z=0.40$, its Mode-1 period was 0.699~s, the largest validated Story-1 EQx shear was 532.489~kN, its governing ULS drift ratio was 0.004784, and its first-peak pushover base shear was 1836.338~kN. The Regular building also had the largest performance-point base shear of 456.253~kN. These quantities describe distinct aspects of seismic demand, deformation and nonlinear capacity and are not interchangeable.

The six plan-irregular models did not exhibit a uniform increase or decrease in response relative to the Regular benchmark. The U-shaped configuration controlled the maximum reported ULS drift ratio (0.004954), first-peak pushover base shear (1882.070~kN in the Y direction), and maximum performance-point roof displacement (27.072~mm in the X direction). In contrast, the Non-Parallel configuration exhibited the largest reported main-storey CM--CR separation (approximately 1.237~m, Story~2, Soil C/$Z=0.25$). Other irregular models fell below the Regular reference for several measures. This variation makes it necessary to interpret each metric with its model, case, direction and selection basis rather than assign one overall geometric ranking.

The complete drift profiles, CM--CR coordinate results, pushover curves and performance-point comparisons likewise distinguish changes in geometry from a formal code-based torsional classification. L2 and Non-Parallel show larger CM--CR separations than the Regular model but lower governing drift and performance-point roof displacement; U is comparatively close to the Regular model in validated EQx shear while exceeding it in selected deformation and capacity measures. CM--CR separation alone is not the NBC diaphragm-end displacement ratio needed to establish torsional irregularity.

The investigated NBC 205:2024 models therefore do not justify unrestricted extension of prescriptive RUD provisions to all irregular plans. A response measure remaining within an adopted analytical limit does not, by itself, establish that a building lies within the geometric scope of ready-to-use detailing. The selected irregular configurations and the separate four-storey height-extension case require project-specific engineered assessment. In particular, a matched authoritative four-storey storey-force series was not available, so this study cannot quantify a height effect on storey-force distribution.

The study-defined area-deficit Geometric Irregularity Index (GII) is useful for describing the investigated plan areas, but not sufficient as a stand-alone performance criterion. L and U each have GII=16.67\% yet differ in shear, drift, pushover and performance-point response. Similarly, L1, L2 and T each have GII=33.33\% but exhibit different responses. The comparison demonstrates that an equal area deficit need not imply equivalent seismic performance, without establishing a universal GII threshold or causal rule.

The modal observations reinforce this need for a response-specific comparison: for Soil Type C at $Z=0.40$, the seven-model fundamental periods range from 0.649 to 0.707~s. Mode~1 contributes approximately 79.09--80.32\% of translational mass in X and Mode~2 approximately 74.82--80.42\% in Y, while cumulative participation exceeds 90\% by Mode~4 in X and Mode~5 in Y for all seven configurations. Taken together, the investigated responses provide bounded evidence about regular and selected irregular low-rise buildings, not a general code acceptance of every irregular RUD layout.

\section{Recommendations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm,itemsep=0.45em,topsep=0.3em,label=\textbullet]
\item Irregular buildings should be subjected to project-specific structural analysis and design rather than treated as automatically covered by the RUD provisions for regular buildings.
\item Where torsional irregularity must be classified formally, the required diaphragm-edge displacement results should be evaluated directly in accordance with the applicable seismic code provisions.
\item A wider range of plan configurations, degrees of irregularity, building heights, soil conditions, and seismic demand levels should be investigated before proposing any extension of the prescriptive scope of NBC 205:2024.
\item Future studies should evaluate additional configurations using consistent modelling assumptions and clearly defined response measures so that the influence of geometry can be distinguished from other structural parameters.
\end{itemize}
