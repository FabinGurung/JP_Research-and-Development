\chapter[CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS]{Conclusions and Recommendations}

\section{Conclusions}
The representative NBC~205:2012-designed building exhibited increasing concrete design-check deficiencies under the investigated NBC~105:2025 demand cases. The findings apply to the assessed model, not to all existing buildings designed under the earlier provisions.

For the NBC~205:2024 comparisons, the Regular model governed the recorded Story-1 EQx storey shear (532.489~kN) and maximum performance-point base shear (456.253~kN). The U-shaped model governed the maximum reported ULS drift ratio (0.004954), first-peak pushover base shear (1882.070~kN) and maximum performance-point roof displacement (27.072~mm). The Non-Parallel model showed the largest governing CM--CR separation (approximately 1.237~m). These measures do not constitute one common capacity or code-eligibility test.

The investigated plan irregularities therefore produce different critical responses rather than a uniform increase or decrease in seismic demand. The study-defined Geometric Irregularity Index describes area deficit but does not provide a stand-alone performance or torsional-irregularity criterion. Formal torsional classification still requires diaphragm-edge displacement evidence; the separate four-storey extension also requires its own engineered assessment.

Accordingly, the available analysis supports building-specific assessment of the selected irregular configurations instead of automatic extension of NBC~205 ready-to-use detailing beyond its documented scope. Complete numerical verification, code applicability checks and the stated reviewer acceptance gates remain separate requirements.

\section{Recommendations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm,itemsep=0.45em,topsep=0.3em,label=\textbullet]
\item Irregular buildings should be subjected to project-specific structural analysis and design rather than treated as automatically covered by the RUD provisions for regular buildings.
\item Where torsional irregularity must be classified formally, the required diaphragm-edge displacement results should be evaluated directly in accordance with the applicable seismic code provisions.
\item A wider range of plan configurations, degrees of irregularity, building heights, soil conditions, and seismic demand levels should be investigated before proposing any extension of the prescriptive scope of NBC 205:2024.
\item Future studies should evaluate additional configurations using consistent modelling assumptions and clearly defined response measures so that the influence of geometry can be distinguished from other structural parameters.
\end{itemize}
