\chapter[CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS]{Conclusions and Recommendations}

\section{Conclusions}
The study evaluated the seismic performance of low-rise reinforced concrete buildings designed using NBC 205 provisions under the seismic demands specified by NBC 105:2025. The analysis included a representative building designed according to Draft Final NBC 205:2012, a regular NBC 205:2024 RUD reference building, six selected plan-irregular configurations, and a separate four-storey height-extension case. The results provide useful evidence on both the performance of the existing simplified detailing provisions and the potential for applying the RUD approach to selected irregular building configurations.

The representative building designed according to Draft Final NBC 205:2012 showed an increase in deficient frame members as the seismic demand increased. The number of failing frames increased from 1 under Soil Type C with a zone factor of 0.25 to 21 under Soil Type C with zone factors of 0.35 and 0.40, while 18 failing frames were observed under Soil Type D with a zone factor of 0.35. These results highlight the importance of reassessing existing buildings designed using earlier provisions against the current seismic demands of NBC 105:2025. However, because the assessment was based on one representative building, the results should not be generalized to all buildings designed according to NBC 205:2012.

The regular NBC 205:2024 building provided the reference for evaluating the six irregular configurations. Under Soil Type C with a zone factor of 0.40, the fundamental periods of the seven configurations ranged from 0.649 to 0.707 seconds. The regular building recorded the highest first-storey shear in the X direction, at 532.489 kN, and the highest performance-point base shear of 456.253 kN. The irregular configurations did not show a uniform reduction in seismic performance compared with the regular building. In particular, the U-shaped building recorded the highest ULS drift ratio of 0.004954, which remained below the adopted NBC 105:2025 limit of 0.025. It also recorded the highest first-peak pushover base shear of 1882.070 kN and the largest performance-point roof displacement of 27.072 mm. The Non-Parallel configuration showed the largest reported CM--CR separation of approximately 1.237 m. These results demonstrate that plan irregularity does not necessarily result in poorer response for every seismic performance parameter and that different configurations may govern different aspects of structural behaviour.

An important outcome of the study is that several selected irregular configurations achieved acceptable values for the investigated seismic response parameters while using the same NBC 205:2024 RUD provisions as the regular reference building. The drift results, pushover response, performance-point response, and comparative force and deformation measures provide positive analytical evidence that the RUD detailing approach may have potential for application beyond the regular building configurations for which it is currently intended. Thus, the study provides a basis for considering the extension of the RUD approach to selected irregular low-rise RC buildings rather than limiting the approach solely to regular configurations. However, the present results are limited to the investigated building configurations and seismic demand cases and should be considered as evidence of potential rather than as a direct code-level validation of such an extension.

The CM--CR separation results further showed that the Non-Parallel configuration had the largest reported plan eccentricity, but these values were used only as a descriptive measure because the storey-end displacement data required for formal torsional-irregularity classification were not available. The available final-step hinge observations similarly provide evidence of the structural response at the last recorded analysis step but do not establish the hinge condition at the performance point. These limitations should therefore be considered when interpreting the nonlinear response results.

The Geometric Irregularity Index (GII) did not show a unique or monotonic relationship with the seismic response of the investigated configurations. Buildings with similar GII values produced different storey shear, drift, pushover, and performance-point responses. GII is therefore useful for describing plan-area irregularity but is not sufficient by itself to predict seismic performance. A more reliable assessment of irregular buildings should consider GII together with storey shear, inter-storey drift, CM--CR eccentricity, torsional response where available, pushover capacity, performance-point response, and hinge behaviour.

The separate four-storey model showed that extending the RUD approach beyond the investigated three-storey configurations requires additional verification. The result provides useful evidence for examining the height limitation of the guideline, but a single four-storey model is not sufficient to establish a general height-effect relationship or a universal four-storey applicability limit.

Overall, the study demonstrates that the selected irregular buildings can exhibit satisfactory seismic response under the investigated NBC 105:2025 demand cases. This finding suggests that the current RUD approach, although developed for regular low-rise buildings, has a potential for extension to certain types of plan-irregular buildings. Such an extension could make the simplified RUD approach more useful for practical low-rise construction in Nepal, where irregular building configurations are common. At the same time, the variation in response among different plan configurations shows that such an extension should be supported by further studies involving a wider range of plan configurations, structural characteristics, and seismic demand cases to establish the limits and conditions. The findings therefore provide a useful basis for future refinement and possible expnasion of the NBC 205 RUD provisions while maintaining appropriate limits and verification requirements for irregular buildings.

\section{Recommendations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=0.8cm,itemsep=0.45em,topsep=0.3em,label=\textbullet]
\item Irregular buildings should be subjected to project-specific structural analysis and design rather than treated as automatically covered by the RUD provisions for regular buildings.
\item Where torsional irregularity must be classified formally, the required diaphragm-edge displacement results should be evaluated directly in accordance with the applicable seismic code provisions.
\item A wider range of plan configurations, degrees of irregularity, building heights, soil conditions, and seismic demand levels should be investigated before proposing any extension of the prescriptive scope of NBC 205:2024.
\item Future studies should evaluate additional configurations using consistent modelling assumptions and clearly defined response measures so that the influence of geometry can be distinguished from other structural parameters.
\end{itemize}
