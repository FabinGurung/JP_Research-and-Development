\justifying
\PUAbstractParagraph{This study evaluates the seismic performance of low-rise reinforced concrete (RC) buildings in Nepal and examines the applicability of the NBC 205 Ready-to-Use Detailing (RUD) guidelines. A representative building designed according to Draft Final NBC 205:2012 was reviewed using the available concrete design-check results. The main analysis considered seven building configurations designed using NBC 205:2024: one regular building and six irregular buildings with L, L1, L2, T, U, and non-parallel plans. Each building consisted of three full storeys and a stair cover. An additional four-storey building was also analysed to examine the effect of extending the building height.}
\PUAbstractParagraph{The buildings were evaluated using the seismic demands specified in NBC 105:2025 for different zone factors and Soil Types C and D. The assessment considered natural periods, modal mass participation, storey shear, inter-storey drift, separation between the centre of mass and centre of rigidity (CM--CR), pushover capacity, performance-point response, and available hinge observations. The available concrete design-check results were also used to assess the representative NBC 205:2012-designed building.}
\PUAbstractParagraph{The main comparisons were conducted under Soil Types C and D, including zone factors of 0.30, 0.35, and 0.40. For Soil Type C with a zone factor of 0.40, the fundamental periods ranged from 0.649 to 0.707~s. The regular building recorded the highest first-storey shear in the X direction, at 532.489~kN. The U-shaped building exhibited the highest ultimate limit state drift ratio of 0.004954, below the NBC 105:2025 limit of 0.025. It also showed the highest first-peak pushover base shear of 1882.070~kN and the largest performance-point roof displacement of 27.072~mm. The regular building recorded the highest performance-point base shear of 456.253~kN, while the non-parallel building had the largest CM--CR separation of 1.237~m. Different plan configurations therefore produced critical responses in different seismic performance parameters.}
\PUAbstractParagraph{The concrete design checks for the representative NBC 205:2012 building showed an increase in deficient frame members as seismic demand increased. The number of failing frames increased from 1 under Soil Type C with a zone factor of 0.25 to 21 under Soil Type C with zone factors of 0.35 and 0.40. For Soil Type D with a zone factor of 0.35, 18 failing frames were observed. These findings highlight the need to reassess existing buildings designed according to earlier provisions against NBC 105:2025 seismic demands.}
\PUAbstractParagraph{The Geometric Irregularity Index (GII) alone was insufficient to explain the differences in seismic responses among the investigated building configurations. Plan irregularities should therefore be assessed using complementary parameters, including storey drift, storey shear, torsional effects, and lateral load-carrying capacity, rather than a single geometric index. The findings indicate potential for extending NBC 205:2024 RUD provisions from regular buildings to certain irregular configurations. However, project-specific seismic evaluation and verification are necessary to establish their suitability and the limitations of such an extension.}
