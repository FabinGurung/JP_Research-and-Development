\justifying
\PUAbstractParagraph{This thesis evaluates the seismic response of low-rise reinforced concrete buildings in Nepal and the applicability limits of NBC 205 Ready-to-Use Detailing (RUD). A representative building designed to Draft Final NBC 205:2012 is assessed using recorded concrete design checks. The NBC 205:2024 comparison comprises one Regular and six plan-irregular configurations (L, L1, L2, T, U and Non-Parallel), each with three full storeys and a stair cover. A separate four-storey Regular extension is excluded from the matched seven-model performance-point comparison.}
\PUAbstractParagraph{The analyses use five NBC 105:2025 demand cases: Soil C with zone factors $Z=0.25$, 0.30, 0.35 and 0.40, and Soil D with $Z=0.35$. Modal behaviour, storey shear, inter-storey drift, centre-of-mass--centre-of-rigidity (CM--CR) separation, pushover capacity, performance points, recorded final-step hinge observations and concrete design checks are compared. Under Soil C, $Z=0.40$, the seven-model fundamental periods range from 0.649 to 0.707~s.}
\PUAbstractParagraph{For Soil C, $Z=0.40$, the Regular model records the highest validated Story-1 EQx storey shear (532.489~kN) and performance-point shear (456.253~kN, X direction). The U-shaped model governs the maximum reported ULS drift ratio (0.004954, below the adopted 0.025 limit), first-peak pushover shear (1882.070~kN, Y direction) and performance-point roof displacement (27.072~mm, X direction). The Non-Parallel model has the largest main-storey CM--CR separation (approximately 1.237~m, Story~2, Soil C, $Z=0.25$); this is not a formal torsional-irregularity classification. The 2012 reference has 1, 15, 21 and 21 deficient frames for Soil C with $Z=0.25$, 0.30, 0.35 and 0.40, respectively, and 18 for Soil D, $Z=0.35$.}
\PUAbstractParagraph{The study-defined Geometric Irregularity Index (GII) does not yield a unique seismic-performance ranking: configurations with equal area deficits can respond differently. Some irregular models satisfy individual investigated response checks, indicating scope for further evaluation of RUD applicability, but not establishing code eligibility or approval of an RUD extension. Project-specific engineering verification remains necessary. The reported findings support case-specific engineering checks for the selected building configurations under the adopted seismic demands.}
