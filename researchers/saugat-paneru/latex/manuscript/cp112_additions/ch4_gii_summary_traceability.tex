\subsection{Interpretation of the Comparative Findings}
The assessment of the earlier NBC 205:2012-designed building and the current NBC 205:2024 models highlights two related aspects of seismic performance. In the earlier reference building, the number of concrete frame design-check failures increased from one under Soil Type C, $Z=0.25$, to 21 under both Soil Type C, $Z=0.35$ and $Z=0.40$. This shows the importance of examining the adopted seismic demand rather than relying solely on the original design basis.

For the seven current building configurations, the critical response depends on the quantity being examined. The Regular model recorded the highest performance-point base shear (456.253~kN), while the U-shaped model recorded the highest governing drift ratio (0.004954), first-peak pushover base shear (1882.070~kN) and performance-point roof displacement (27.072~mm). The Non-Parallel configuration had the greatest resultant CM--CR separation (approximately 1.237~m). These contrasting results show why strength, deformation and eccentricity should be considered together when comparing irregular plans.

The GII comparison provides a further distinction: models with the same plan-area reduction do not necessarily respond in the same way. The L- and U-shaped models each have a GII of 16.67\%, yet their performance-point responses differ. Similar differences occur among the L1-, L2- and T-shaped models, all with a GII of 33.33\%. Consequently, an area-based description of irregularity is useful for comparing geometry but cannot, by itself, establish seismic performance or the suitability of RUD provisions. The separate four-storey case is considered as a design-check observation rather than part of the matched seven-configuration comparison.
