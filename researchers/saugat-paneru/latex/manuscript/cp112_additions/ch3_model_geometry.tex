\subsection{Geometric Irregularity Index (GII)}
For the plan comparison, the Geometric Irregularity Index (GII) is the percentage plan-area deficit relative to the corresponding bounding rectangle. Figure~\ref{fig:gii_definition} shows the definition schematically for a re-entrant plan. The missing/re-entrant area is a geometric descriptor only; the location and topology of the missing area are retained separately through the plan type, re-entrant projections and corner count.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.75,font=\small]
\draw[very thick] (0,0) rectangle (8,5);
\fill[gray!25] (5,3) rectangle (8,5);
\draw[very thick] (0,0)--(8,0)--(8,3)--(5,3)--(5,5)--(0,5)--cycle;
\draw[dashed] (0,0) rectangle (8,5);
\node at (3.2,2.3) {$A_p$ = actual plan area};
\node[align=center] at (6.5,4.0) {removed /\\re-entrant area};
\node[below] at (4,0) {bounding rectangle area $A_b$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\caption{Plan-area description used for the Geometric Irregularity Index (GII).}
\label{fig:gii_definition}
\end{figure}

The schematic in Figure~\ref{fig:gii_definition} defines GII as the percentage difference between the bounding-rectangle area and the actual plan area. The index therefore measures plan-area deficit only; it does not identify the location, direction or topology of the irregular portion.
