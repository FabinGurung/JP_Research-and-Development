\chapter{RESULTS AND DISCUSSION}

\section{General}

The seismic response of the six structural blocks of the Infectious and Communicable Disease Hospital was evaluated for fixed-base, hard-, medium- and soft-soil support conditions. Modal characteristics and response-spectrum demands were considered alongside the foundation reactions and member forces obtained from the gravity-load combination. Comparisons were made separately for each block because the structural dimensions and dynamic properties differ among the blocks.

Displacements, inter-storey drift and shear forces were evaluated for the relevant horizontal response-spectrum cases. Foundation settlements and structural member forces were assessed under $1.2DL+1.5LL$, providing complementary information on the effect of foundation flexibility under gravity loading.

\subsection*{Directional and torsional participation}
Reported participating-mass ratios describe coupled modes and do not independently establish pure translational or torsional mode shapes. Interpretation of directional modes requires verification of the model assignments.

For Block~4, the cumulative UX and UY ratios after mode~8 are 90.39\% and 90.54\% for fixed support, compared with 89.79\% and 89.93\% for hard support; in the hard case both criteria are met at mode~10, with 90.70\% UX and 90.03\% UY. Thus the increase from eight to ten modes follows the reported participation threshold and does not by itself indicate poorer structural performance. The participation-based classification does not establish the spatial mode shapes.

In a simple uncoupled sway--rocking idealization, foundation translation and rotation add compliance to the fixed-base structural flexibility, so that $\widetilde T/T\approx[1+k/K_h+kh_e^2/K_r]^{1/2}$, where $k$ is structural lateral stiffness, $h_e$ effective height, and $K_h$ and $K_r$ the horizontal and rocking foundation stiffnesses \cite{veletsos1974,nist2012ssi}. This provides a qualitative explanation for different SSI sensitivities. Block~2 has the largest reported first-mode change, 19.73\%, but identifying its cause requires directional mode-shape tracking, effective mass/height and validated footing stiffness. It is not established by period lengthening alone.

The two shear tables require consistent case definitions, scale factors, modal combination and extraction locations before their differences can be attributed to SSI. For Block~2 fixed support, the X base reaction is 6,578.8~kN while the maximum storey shear is 6,113.3~kN, a difference of 465.5~kN (7.1\% of the base reaction). Nearly unchanged base shear could reflect normalization or spectrum shape; neither cause is established. These results therefore do not yet support a conclusion that seismic force demand is unchanged by period lengthening. The design spectrum and case-specific scale factors are required for a quantitative reconciliation.

\section{Comparison with Published Soil--Structure Interaction Studies}

Dhakal and Chaulagain \cite{dhakal2023} examined regular and plan-irregular reinforced-concrete buildings supported on hard, medium and soft soils using response-spectrum analysis and a linear Winkler foundation representation. They reported longer fundamental periods and greater storey displacement and drift when soil flexibility was included, together with a reduction in base shear in their studied configurations. The present six-block hospital assessment shows the same overall period-lengthening and displacement trends. However, the base-shear results do not show a consistent reduction in every block. This difference is important: the force response depends on the structural configuration, support idealization and particular response measure, not on period lengthening alone.

Poudel and Chaulagain \cite{poudel2024} examined irregular reinforced-concrete buildings with fully nonlinear soil--structure interaction and dynamic analysis. Their results emphasize that fixed-base and linearized soil assumptions can underestimate some structural demands, especially under softer ground conditions. Increased displacement and drift in the present flexible-support cases are consistent with that broader concern. The two analyses are nevertheless not directly equivalent. The hospital models use discrete foundation springs for the soil--structure interaction comparison, whereas the published study explicitly evaluates nonlinear soil effects. The two studies therefore provide a useful check on the direction of response trends, but not a numerical calibration or independent verification of this hospital model.

In the present work, the response-spectrum displacement, drift and shear results are interpreted separately from the gravity-combination foundation settlements and member-force envelopes. Likewise, the twelve-ground-motion threshold-intensity data and the X-direction block-specific fragility curves are discussed according to their respective definitions; published studies of irregular buildings do not by themselves validate these hospital-specific threshold values. Taken together, the comparison supports evaluating SSI through several response parameters rather than assuming a uniform beneficial or detrimental change for all six blocks.

\section{Overall Discussion}

The response parameters show that foundation flexibility influences the dynamic and deformation characteristics of the hospital blocks to differing degrees. The effect on fundamental period is systematic, whereas the effects on structural forces are dependent on the response parameter and loading condition.

For Block~1, Y drift falls from 0.467\% to 0.446\% at medium support while uppermost-storey Y displacement increases; a change in deformation profile is consistent with this combination, but its modal mechanism requires storey-by-storey verification. The gravity beam-shear envelope falls by 1.8\% in Block~4 and 4.5\% in Block~6 from fixed to soft support. Foundation flexibility can redistribute gravity actions and change the governing member; without tracking the member stations, these decreases cannot be interpreted as a general seismic benefit.

\subsection*{Consistency of response comparisons}
The interpretation distinguishes response-spectrum results from the separate gravity-combination outputs. Values are compared only within the corresponding block, support condition, response component and loading case; horizontal and vertical quantities are not combined. The foundation and member-force comparisons employ the $1.2DL+1.5LL$ load combination, while seismic displacements, drift and shear are evaluated using the respective response-spectrum cases. The response comparisons represent the specified structural models and support assumptions rather than direct measurements of the completed hospital.

Across all six blocks, reduced foundation restraint is associated with greater fundamental periods and higher X-direction uppermost-storey displacements. Maximum drifts also vary with soil stiffness and structural configuration. In contrast, base shear and member forces do not follow a single monotonic pattern. The comparison highlights the importance of examining displacement, drift, shear, foundation movement and member demand together when assessing soil--structure interaction. The reconstructed fragility parameters supplement these deterministic comparisons, subject to their extrapolated thresholds, assumed uncertainty and unresolved nonlinear-model basis.
