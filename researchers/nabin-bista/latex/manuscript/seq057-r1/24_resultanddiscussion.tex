\chapter{RESULTS AND DISCUSSION}

\section{General}

The seismic response of the six structural blocks of the Infectious and Communicable Disease Hospital was evaluated for fixed-base, hard-, medium- and soft-soil support conditions. Modal characteristics and response-spectrum demands were considered alongside the foundation reactions and member forces obtained from the gravity-load combination. Comparisons were made separately for each block because the structural dimensions and dynamic properties differ among the blocks.

Displacements, inter-storey drift and shear forces were evaluated for the relevant horizontal response-spectrum cases. Foundation settlements and structural member forces were assessed under $1.2DL+1.5LL$, providing complementary information on the effect of foundation flexibility under gravity loading.

\subsection*{Directional and torsional participation}
Reported participating-mass ratios describe coupled modes and do not independently establish pure translational or torsional mode shapes. Directional mode interpretation also depends on the modal assignments and corresponding mode shapes.

For Block~4, the cumulative UX and UY ratios after mode~8 are 90.39\% and 90.54\% for fixed support, compared with 89.79\% and 89.93\% for hard support; in the hard case both criteria are met at mode~10, with 90.70\% UX and 90.03\% UY. Thus the increase from eight to ten modes follows the reported participation threshold and does not by itself indicate poorer structural performance. The participation-based classification does not establish the spatial mode shapes.

In a simple uncoupled sway--rocking idealization, foundation translation and rotation add compliance to the fixed-base structural flexibility, so that $\widetilde T/T\approx[1+k/K_h+kh_e^2/K_r]^{1/2}$, where $k$ is structural lateral stiffness, $h_e$ effective height, and $K_h$ and $K_r$ the horizontal and rocking foundation stiffnesses \cite{veletsos1974,nist2012ssi}. This provides a qualitative explanation for different SSI sensitivities. Block~2 has the largest reported first-mode change, 19.73\%, but identifying its cause requires directional mode-shape tracking, effective mass/height and validated footing stiffness. It is not established by period lengthening alone.

The two shear tables require consistent case definitions, scale factors, modal combination and extraction locations before their differences can be attributed to SSI. For Block~2 fixed support, the X base reaction is 6,578.8~kN while the maximum storey shear is 6,113.3~kN, a difference of 465.5~kN (7.1\% of the base reaction). The design spectrum and case-specific scale factors are required for a quantitative reconciliation.

\section{Comparison with Published Soil--Structure Interaction Studies}

The three investigations address foundation flexibility, but they employ different structural systems and analysis methods. Dhakal and Chaulagain~\cite{dhakal2023} studied five building-plan configurations (regular, T-, L-, H- and plus-shaped) under hard, medium and soft support with linear Winkler springs and response-spectrum analysis. Poudel and Chaulagain~\cite{poudel2024} considered six regular and irregular configurations under fixed, linear-soil and nonlinear-soil conditions, using three earthquake records in nonlinear time-history analysis. The present study evaluates six blocks of a G+4 hospital using fixed-base and three elastic-spring support conditions. These differences govern which numerical comparisons are meaningful.

\subsection*{Published benchmarks}

For Dhakal and Chaulagain~\cite{dhakal2023}, the accessible published results establish increases in fundamental period, storey displacement and drift, together with reduced base shear when soil flexibility is introduced. Their publicly accessible abstract identifies the five plan types and three soil categories, but does not give numerical percentages for those response changes. Consequently, a numerical difference relative to that study cannot be calculated from the accessible figures and text without an authenticated copy of the full results. No unreported percentage is assigned to it here.

Poudel and Chaulagain~\cite{poudel2024} give explicit percentage changes relative to fixed-base response. For nonlinear soft soil, the mean X-direction top-storey displacement increased by 85.35\% under the Gorkha record, 88.68\% under Northridge and 65.38\% under San Fernando. Under the same three records, the average normalized base-shear increases for nonlinear soft soil were 81.78\%, 79.91\% and 59.29\%, respectively. Their percentages were averaged across the six investigated building configurations, and describe dynamic response with nonlinear soil, not the elastic response-spectrum demands of this hospital. Table~\ref{tab:seq080_published_benchmarks} records these numerical reference values alongside the scope of the Dhakal and Chaulagain study.

\subsection*{Hospital results against the benchmarks}

The six hospital blocks show fixed-to-soft increases in fundamental period of 8.53\%, 19.73\%, 5.22\%, 8.99\%, 9.00\% and 7.85\%, in block order (Table~\ref{tab:seq54_modal}). The corresponding X-direction uppermost-storey displacement increases are 86.8\%, 30.8\%, 7.5\%, 17.0\%, 22.4\% and 16.8\%. These are within-block differences, not averages over different earthquake records. The largest increase, 86.8\% for Block~1, corresponds to 59.39~mm under fixed support and 110.92~mm under soft support in Table~\ref{tab:seq54_disp}. The six-block X-direction increase spans 7.5--86.8\%, and the Y-direction increase spans 8.6--63.5\%. Direct arithmetic using Block~1 values gives $(110.92-59.39)/59.39\times100=86.77\%$, which rounds to the 86.8\% displayed in the hospital results; the corresponding Block~2 period change is $(0.698-0.583)/0.583\times100=19.73\%$. These checks establish consistency of the reported percentage calculations, not independent verification of the underlying ETABS models.

The hospital's maximum X-direction drift changes by +6.5\% to +45.8\% across the six blocks, while its Y-direction drift changes range from $-4.1$\% to +47.4\% (Table~\ref{tab:seq066_fixed_soft}). Its response-spectrum X-direction base-shear changes range from +1.4\% to +10.1\%; the Y-direction changes range from +1.5\% to +11.2\%. The positive X-direction base-shear changes do not reproduce the reduction reported by Dhakal and Chaulagain, but are in the same direction as the increase observed in Poudel and Chaulagain's nonlinear dynamic cases. They are far smaller than that latter study's +59.29--+81.78\% nonlinear-soft values. Different buildings, excitation procedures, definitions of base shear and soil idealizations preclude treating these differences as errors or as numerical validation of the hospital model.

\section{Overall Discussion}

The response parameters show that foundation flexibility influences the dynamic and deformation characteristics of the hospital blocks to differing degrees. The effect on fundamental period is systematic, whereas the effects on structural forces are dependent on the response parameter and loading condition.

Foundation flexibility can redistribute gravity actions and change the governing member; without tracking the member stations, these decreases cannot be interpreted as a general seismic benefit.

\subsection*{Consistency of response comparisons}
The interpretation distinguishes response-spectrum results from the separate gravity-combination outputs. Values are compared only within the corresponding block, support condition, response component and loading case; horizontal and vertical quantities are not combined. The foundation and member-force comparisons employ the $1.2DL+1.5LL$ load combination, while seismic displacements, drift and shear are evaluated using the respective response-spectrum cases. The response comparisons represent the specified structural models and support assumptions rather than direct measurements of the completed hospital.

Across all six blocks, reduced foundation restraint is associated with greater fundamental periods and higher X-direction uppermost-storey displacements. Maximum drifts also vary with soil stiffness and structural configuration. In contrast, base shear and member forces do not follow a single monotonic pattern. The comparison highlights the importance of examining displacement, drift, shear, foundation movement and member demand together when assessing soil--structure interaction. The reconstructed fragility parameters supplement these deterministic comparisons, subject to their extrapolated thresholds, assumed uncertainty and unresolved nonlinear-model basis.
