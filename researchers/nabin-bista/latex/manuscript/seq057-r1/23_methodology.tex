\chapter{METHODOLOGY}
\section{Study Design}
The assessment follows the sequence of model definition, support-spring representation, modal comparison, response-spectrum analysis, gravity-load response examination and fragility interpretation. This ordering relates each research question to its corresponding engineering demand parameter and preserves a consistent baseline for comparing the four support cases.

The six structural blocks of the Infectious and Communicable Disease Hospital were analysed for fixed-base, hard-soil, medium-soil and soft-soil support conditions. Modal, response-spectrum and gravity-load results were compared to evaluate the influence of foundation flexibility on natural period, displacement, inter-storey drift, shear, settlement and member forces. Fragility relationships were considered as a complementary measure of seismic performance.

\section{Numerical Model}
The three-dimensional reinforced-concrete numerical models of the hospital blocks were developed using ETABS v22, a finite-element-based structural analysis program. The six blocks shown in the architectural plan were represented separately. Structural members include beams, columns and slabs, with shear-wall elements where indicated in the submitted structural dimensions. Member sections were assigned according to Table~\ref{tab:member_dimensions_methodology}; the foundation-support properties follow Table~\ref{tab:spring_stiffness_methodology}. Building geometry, imposed gravity loads and seismic loading definitions were taken from the model input schedules.

For each block, the fixed-base idealization restrains foundation movement and provides the reference response. Flexible-base cases represent hard, medium and soft soil using directional translational and rotational foundation springs. In generalized coordinates, the foundation stiffness may be expressed as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{K}_f=\operatorname{diag}(K_x,K_y,K_z,K_{\theta x},K_{\theta y},K_t),
\label{eq:foundation_six_dof}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Six-degree-of-freedom foundation spring matrix}
where the first three terms represent translational restraint and the final three represent rocking and torsion about the relevant local axes. This diagonal description is the idealized uncoupled six-degree-of-freedom spring model; it should not be interpreted as a measured full impedance matrix for the as-built foundation.

The Winkler idealization relates soil reaction locally to displacement, whereas the equivalent directional foundation impedances are informed by the Gazetas (1991) elastic-foundation framework \cite{wolf1985,gazetas1991}. The reported input schedules provide the actual six spring-stiffness components for each block and soil condition. The specified stiffness coefficients define the hard-, medium- and soft-soil analytical cases; these models idealize the supporting soil through linear elastic springs. Figure~\ref{fig:winkler_gazetas_schematic} illustrates the mechanical distinction.

\subsection{Analysis cases and response quantities}
Modal analysis provides natural periods and cumulative participating mass ratios in the two horizontal directions. Response-spectrum analysis gives the horizontal displacement, inter-storey drift, base reaction and storey-shear response for the specified X and Y cases. The independent $1.2DL+1.5LL$ gravity combination provides base-joint settlement, vertical reaction and beam/column member-force components. Each set of results is tabulated against its own load-case definition to prevent the erroneous use of gravity results as earthquake results. The X-direction fragility relationships are examined separately from the elastic response-spectrum maxima.

\subsection{Consistency checks}
Numerical comparison proceeds in three stages: first, confirm the block and support-case identity of every result; second, preserve each component's physical unit, direction and loading-case definition; third, compare trends within a block rather than across unrelated geometries. Comparisons retain each block's original analytical identity and loading condition. The assessment is based on the defined analytical models rather than a direct measurement of the completed hospital.

Within the Winkler idealization, contact pressure is proportional to local vertical soil displacement. The one-parameter relationship is
\begin{equation}
p(x,y)=k_s\,w(x,y),
\label{eq:winkler_soil_reaction}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Local vertical soil reaction in the Winkler idealization}
where $p(x,y)$ is contact pressure, $w(x,y)$ is local foundation settlement, and $k_s$ is the modulus of subgrade reaction. The six-component foundation representation additionally accounts for horizontal translation, rocking and torsion. Equivalent directional stiffness relations for rigid foundations on an elastic half-space are discussed by Gazetas~\cite{gazetas1991}.

\section{Foundation-Support Representation}
In the fixed-base model, foundation translation and rotation are restrained. In the three flexible-base cases, foundation supports are represented by translational, rocking and torsional springs. Each soil condition is assigned its own stiffness components, as described by the six-degree-of-freedom representation in Equation~\ref{eq:foundation_six_dof}. The discrete-spring representation follows the substructure approach to soil--structure interaction~\cite{wolf1985,gazetas1991}.

For a rectangular footing with plan dimensions $2L\times2B$, let $A_b$ denote the contact area and $\chi=A_b/(4L^2)=B/L$. The static elastic-half-space expressions adopted for the directional foundation stiffnesses are~\cite{gazetas1991}
\begin{equation}
K_z = \frac{2GL}{1-\nu}\left(0.73+1.54\chi^{0.75}\right),\label{eq:gazetas_kz}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Vertical foundation stiffness}
\begin{equation}
K_x=K_y = \frac{2GL}{2-\nu}\left(2+2.50\chi^{0.85}\right),\label{eq:gazetas_kxy}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Horizontal foundation stiffness}
\begin{equation}
K_{\theta x} = \frac{G}{1-\nu}I_{bx}^{0.75}\left(\frac{L}{B}\right)^{0.25}\left(2.4+0.5\frac{B}{L}\right),\label{eq:gazetas_krx}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Rocking stiffness about the x-axis}
\begin{equation}
K_{\theta y} = \frac{G}{1-\nu}I_{by}^{0.75}\left[3\left(\frac{L}{B}\right)^{0.15}\right],\label{eq:gazetas_kry}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Rocking stiffness about the y-axis}
\begin{equation}
K_t = GJ_b^{0.75}\left[4+11\left(1-\frac{B}{L}\right)^{0.75}\right].\label{eq:gazetas_kt}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Torsional foundation stiffness}
Here $G$ is the soil shear modulus, $\nu$ is Poisson's ratio, $I_{bx}$ and $I_{by}$ are the centroidal second moments of contact area, and $J_b$ is the polar area moment. The directional stiffness expressions provide the elastic foundation formulation; the ETABS assignments used for the response comparisons are tabulated separately below.

\section{Modal Response Extraction and Comparison}
Modal analysis results were evaluated separately for each block and foundation-support condition. The first-mode period $T_{1,b,s}$ is extracted from the tabulated modal results. For every case, the smallest modal number with both horizontal cumulative participating-mass ratios at or above 0.90 is recorded. 

The percentage change in period from fixed to a flexible support case is computed within the same block as
\begin{equation}
\Delta T_{b,s}=100\frac{T_{1,b,s}-T_{1,b,\mathrm{fixed}}}{T_{1,b,\mathrm{fixed}}}\,\%,
\label{eq:ssi_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
\RegisterThesisEquation{Percentage change in fundamental period}
where $b$ identifies the block and $s$ denotes hard, medium or soft support. No percentage comparison is calculated between unrelated blocks or between physically different output quantities.

\subsection{Reconstruction of the existing fragility calculation}
The six block-specific X-direction analysis data tables each contain four support cases, twelve ground-motion response columns and PGA levels from 0 to 0.5~$g$ at 0.1~$g$ increments. Their response columns are labelled displacement and percent ISDR; the displacement normalization and the underlying nonlinear model assignments are not independently established. The record list identifies twelve named events, but does not establish NGA record identifiers, magnitude, distance, $V_{s,30}$, component orientation or record-specific scale factors. The analysis is consequently limited to reconstruction of the data table calculation, rather than reproduction of nonlinear incremental dynamic analysis.

For each record, a least-squares linear forecast is used of PGA against percent ISDR to estimate threshold intensity at 0.5\%, 1\%, 2\% and 4\%. Some threshold intensities exceed the largest analysed level of 0.5~$g$ and are extrapolations. Let $x_j$ be the twelve estimated threshold intensities, $m$ their arithmetic mean and $s$ their population standard deviation. The data table calculates
\[
\beta_r=\sqrt{\ln[1+(s/m)^2]},\qquad
\beta_w=\sqrt{\beta_r^2+0.4^2},\qquad
P_w(IM=x)=\Phi\!\left(\frac{\ln(x/m)}{\beta_w}\right).
\]
The arithmetic mean $m$ is used directly as the curve's 50\% intensity $\theta_w$; this is the implemented data table convention, not a maximum-likelihood estimate of the median of the twelve thresholds. For a moment-matched lognormal distribution the median would instead be $m\exp(-\beta_r^2/2)$. The added 0.4 term is an assumed model-uncertainty contribution, not a dispersion established by nonlinear model validation.

The formulas do not establish nonlinear hinges, yielding sequence or post-yield damage evolution. Extrapolating an elastic trend to a drift threshold cannot demonstrate that the structure sustains that response. The X-only analysis data tables do not justify equivalent performance in Y. Chapter~4 reports the reconstructed parameters while retaining these methodological limits.

\section{Interpretation Procedure}
Results are organized by block number, support case, output quantity, and original load case. For the response-spectrum displacement results, the reported response at the greatest listed storey elevation is extracted separately from the RS-X X-component and RS-Y Y-component tables. For response-spectrum drift, the maximum absolute reported storey drift ratio is identified separately for the same response cases and converted to percent. Base shear is read from the response-spectrum maximum FX (RS-X) and FY (RS-Y) base-reaction rows; the largest absolute response-spectrum storey-shear value is selected from the corresponding storey rows without summation across floors.

Foundation settlements are the largest absolute base-joint Uz displacement, vertical reactions are the FZ component, and beam and column force envelopes are maximum absolute member-force components at the recorded element stations, all under the independent \texttt{1.2DL + 1.5LL} combination. These gravity-combination outputs are not relabelled as response-spectrum results. The controlling member forces were evaluated for each block and support condition. Vertical reactions and displacements are compared only where the support-case identity and load combination are consistent.
