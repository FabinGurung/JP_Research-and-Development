\chapter{CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION}
\section{Conclusions}
For the six structural blocks of one representative mid-size G+4 hospital, the first-mode period increases from fixed-base to soft-soil support by 8.53\%, 19.73\%, 5.22\%, 8.99\%, 9.00\% and 7.85\% for Blocks 1--6, respectively. Block 2 has the largest period change, while Block 3 has the smallest. The reported modal runs attain at least 90\% cumulative mass participation in both horizontal directions within five to ten modes. These changes establish sensitivity to support restraint, not independent confirmation of pure mode shapes or a hospital's safety classification.

Block 1 X-direction uppermost-storey response-spectrum displacement increases from 59.39 mm (fixed) to 110.92 mm (soft), an increase of 86.8\%. The greatest reported inter-storey drift is 0.774\% for Block 3 under soft-soil support. Block 6 Y-direction maximum storey shear increases from 3,465.8 to 3,855.6 kN (approximately 11.2\%), while base-shear figures for several blocks remain nearly constant; the reported base- and storey-shear results cannot be reconciled quantitatively without matching spectral scaling and extraction procedures. Under the separate $1.2DL+1.5LL$ gravity combination, Block 1 maximum base-joint movement increases from 0.592 mm (hard) to 9.925 mm (soft), and the Block 1 beam-shear envelope increases from 233.4 kN (fixed) to 426.4 kN (soft). These gravity results do not represent seismic demand or establish footing-capacity compliance. Block 2 fixed-base settlement still requires clarification.

The reconstructed X-direction 0.5\%-drift threshold intensity decreases from fixed to soft support by 30.9\%, 18.5\%, 7.0\%, 17.0\%, 15.2\% and 27.6\% for Blocks 1--6. In the separate twelve-ground-motion comparison, the operational threshold mean decreases from 0.142 $g$ (fixed) to 0.088 $g$ (soft), approximately 38.0\%. Those record-level means are not the medians of the six individual blocks. The block comparisons are calculated from linear threshold forecasts with an assumed 0.4 model-uncertainty component; they do not establish independently fitted nonlinear fragility curves, physical collapse probabilities, or continued hospital operations.

The investigation concerns one representative hospital divided into six structural blocks; it is not a statistical assessment of hospital buildings throughout Nepal. The hard-, medium- and soft-soil cases are analytical alternatives rather than independently measured site conditions. Linear independent foundation springs omit embedment, uplift, sliding, gapping, radiation damping, kinematic interaction and strain-dependent soil stiffness. Elastic modal/response-spectrum outputs and separately extracted gravity demands do not constitute nonlinear dynamic analysis, capacity assessment or an as-built performance certification. Record identifiers, ground-motion scaling, model constraints, footing dimensions, spectral amplification, and directional nonlinear fragility require further model-specific assessment before design application.

\section{Recommendations}
At sites with potentially soft foundation support, compare fixed-base and flexible-base representations for every structural block; assess lateral displacement, inter-storey drift, shear, and foundation rotation rather than assuming period lengthening is beneficial. Obtain site-specific shear-wave velocity $V_{s,30}$, SPT/CPT and geotechnical test data and use actual footing sizes, embedment and contact pressure to re-estimate consistent translational, rocking and torsional stiffnesses. Check spring assignments and all six components against the original ETABS files and governing foundation geometry.

Document the adopted NBC 105:2025 seismic zone, importance factor, damping, response spectrum, directional scaling and load combinations; reconcile base reactions with storey-shear definitions. For the G+4 hospital, calculate code-consistent amplified drift, nonstructural acceleration, separation/pounding, serviceability and differential settlement, and compare individual footing reactions to appropriate pressure and capacity limits. Assess beam and column seismic demand-capacity ratios separately from the gravity-only force envelopes.

For credible performance-state probabilities, use traceable earthquake components and intensity scaling, nonlinear structural and soil response where feasible, explicit engineering demand parameters and damage criteria, and separately fitted fragility median and dispersion for each block/support case. Validate hospital functional requirements, bed classification, access, egress and lift dimensions against approved drawings rather than interpreting these analytical results as evidence of architectural or post-earthquake operational compliance.
