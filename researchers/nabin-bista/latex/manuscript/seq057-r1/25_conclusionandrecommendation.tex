\chapter{CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATION}
\section{Conclusions}
The six-block analysis shows a consistent influence of foundation flexibility on the modal response. From fixed-base to soft-soil support, the first-mode periods increase by 8.53\%, 19.73\%, 5.22\%, 8.99\%, 9.00\% and 7.85\% for Blocks~1--6, respectively. Block~2 exhibits the greatest period increase. The tabulated modal runs reach 90\% cumulative mass participation in both horizontal directions; the detailed directional mode-shape characteristics require the complete modal output for further interpretation.

Seismic deformation generally increases with softer support. Block~1 X-direction uppermost-storey displacement increases from 59.39 to 110.92~mm (86.8\%). The maximum reported response-spectrum drift is 0.774\% for Block~3 under soft support. By contrast, shear demand does not follow a uniform trend: Block~6 Y-direction maximum storey shear increases by approximately 11.2\%, while several reported base-shear values remain nearly unchanged across support cases. Base-shear normalization and its relationship to the independent storey-shear output require a consistent model-by-model check before force-based design conclusions.

Foundation response under the separate $1.2DL+1.5LL$ gravity combination becomes more pronounced with softer support. Block~1 maximum settlement changes from 0.592~mm under hard-soil support to 9.925~mm under soft-soil support. The twelve-record fragility-threshold dataset gives mean operational intensities of 0.142~$g$ and 0.088~$g$ for fixed and soft support, respectively. These are dataset-level values and are not identified as block-specific X-direction medians. The X-direction block illustrations indicate soil-dependent changes in exceedance response, but a comparable fitted-parameter basis is needed to quantify probabilistic shifts among all six blocks.

\section{Recommendations}
For a site with relatively flexible support, both fixed- and flexible-base models should be considered during seismic assessment. Site-specific $V_s$, SPT and geotechnical data should determine the final spring properties and bearing-capacity checks. The footing dimensions for each block, design load combinations and complete modal and spectral-scaling settings should be confirmed against the ETABS model before design-level decisions.

For hospital functionality, amplified or otherwise code-consistent drift and floor-acceleration checks should accompany structural member demand-capacity checks, differential settlement and foundation bearing pressure. Nonlinear analyses with documented ground-motion selection, scale factors, component response, damage-state criteria, and fragility fitting should be used to assess the probability of damage-state exceedance. The linear-spring comparison of a single hospital is not a substitute for site-specific nonlinear and as-built performance assessment.
